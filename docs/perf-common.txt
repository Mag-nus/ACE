SECTION A - CLOCK-READ STORMS
================================================================================

K.1  Time.GetUnixTime() called once per LANDBLOCK rather than once per tick
     ACE.Common/Time.cs:9-19, called from
     ACE.Server/Managers/LandblockManager.cs:387, 412, 424

     The call sits INSIDE `foreach (var landblock in landblockGroup)`.
     GetUnixTime is DateTime.UtcNow plus a TimeSpan subtract plus a
     TotalSeconds divide. The world loop sleeps 1ms
     (Managers/WorldManager.cs:392), so this runs across hundreds of active
     landblocks at up to ~1000 loop iterations per second.

     FIX: hoist `var now = Time.GetUnixTime();` above each loop.

     BONUS CORRECTNESS WIN: landblocks within a single tick currently observe
     DIFFERENT "now" values, which contradicts the codebase's own treatment of
     Timers.PortalYearTicks as a stable per-frame value.

K.2  Up to 8 UtcNow reads per session per Update()
     ACE.Server/Network/NetworkSession.cs:187, 203, 208, 211, 215, 218, 228, 244

     Update() is driven from NetworkManager.cs:359 (Parallel.ForEach over the
     full sessionMap) every world tick. At 100 sessions and ~1000 ticks/sec
     that is roughly 800,000 clock reads per second. DateTime.UtcNow is
     approximately 20-30ns - it is leap-second aware, not a free rdtsc.

     FIX: one `var now = DateTime.UtcNow;` at the top of Update(). The semantic
     change is sub-millisecond.

     The same pattern appears at ACE.Server/Entity/Landblock.cs:516, 518, 619,
     622, 625, 628 - 4 UtcNow reads per landblock per tick purely for the 5m
     and 1h history-clear checks.


SECTION B - INSTRUMENTATION
================================================================================

K.3  Instrumentation pays its cost even when disabled
     ACE.Server/Managers/ServerPerformanceMonitor.cs:245-252

     AddToCumulativeEvent guards on IsRunning INSIDE THE CALLEE - but the
     argument `stopwatch.Elapsed.TotalSeconds` is evaluated at the CALL SITE
     first.

     Landblock.Tick does this 8 times (Entity/Landblock.cs:438, 453, 473, 493,
     515, 558, 591, 596), each preceded by a stopwatch.Restart(), so roughly 16
     unconditional QueryPerformanceCounter calls per landblock per tick - plus
     Monitor5m/Monitor1h Restart/Pause/Resume/RegisterEventEnd. Each RateMonitor
     wraps its own Stopwatch, and ServerPerformanceMonitor keeps THREE
     RateMonitors per event type, so RegisterEventEnd (lines 223-231) is 3
     stops plus 3 Elapsed reads.

     FIX: expose a cheap IsRunningCumulative check at the call site, or pass
     the Stopwatch and read Elapsed inside the guard.

     See perf-managers.txt P.1-P.4 for the same cluster from the Managers side,
     including the finding that Config.js on this checkout has the monitor
     AUTO-STARTED.


SECTION C - HOT PRIMITIVES
================================================================================

K.4  ThreadSafeRandom pays ThreadLocal<T> indirection on every roll
     ACE.Common/ThreadSafeRandom.cs:10

     All four methods do `random.Value`, which is a slot-array lookup with a
     generation and linked-slot check - roughly 5-10ns, versus about 1ns for a
     plain [ThreadStatic] field read.

     There are 210 call sites in ACE.Server, 90 in Factories/ alone. A single
     loot roll hits it dozens of times (Factories/LootGenerationFactory.cs:48-79
     plus per-item rolls), and combat and enchantment paths call it per swing.

     FIX: Random.Shared (.NET 6+) is thread-safe, lock-free, and removes both
     the ThreadLocal lookup and the per-thread Random allocation.

     *** BLAST RADIUS WARNING: this CHANGES THE RNG STREAM. *** The file's own
     comment at line 6 refers to unit tests - check them before switching.
     Also note the inclusive-bounds contract documented in bugs-common.txt I.2;
     Random.Shared.Next is EXCLUSIVE, so the wrapper's semantics must be
     preserved exactly or every one of those 210 call sites shifts by one.

K.5  Hash32.Calculate is scalar and trivially vectorisable
     ACE.Common/Cryptography/Hash32.cs:8-22, 24-38

     It is a plain sum of little-endian uints - the ideal SIMD shape. It runs
     per packet AND per fragment, in both directions:
       outbound: Network/ServerPacket.cs:58 (body), once per fragment via
                 Network/ServerPacketFragment.cs:26, and its header at
                 PacketFragmentHeader.cs:64
       inbound:  Network/PacketHeader.cs:64, PacketHeaderOptional.cs:133, and
                 ClientPacketFragment.cs:31 (two calls per fragment)

     Two secondary defects in the Span overload: the loop condition
     `i < length && i + 4 <= length` (line 12) is redundant, and `data.Slice(i)`
     (line 13) constructs a new bounds-checked Span every iteration.

     FIX: MemoryMarshal.Cast<byte, uint>(data) for the body, accumulate with
     Vector<uint>, horizontal-add the lanes at the end. Expect 4-8x on the
     dominant loop. It is BIT-EXACT, because unsigned wraparound addition is
     associative.

     Blast radius is contained: the checksum value is unchanged, and
     correctness is directly covered by whether clients can connect at all.

K.6  ISAAC.Next is cheap; CryptoSystem.Search is the one to watch
     ACE.Common/Cryptography/ISAAC.cs:31-48 and CryptoSystem.cs

     ISAAC.Next is an array index plus a decrement, amortising one 256-round
     IsaacScramble over 256 calls - genuinely negligible at one call per
     outbound encrypted packet (NetworkSession.cs:745). The `if (isReleased)`
     branch predicts perfectly.

     The real cost is CryptoSystem.Search, called from Network/ClientPacket.cs:143
     on EVERY encrypted inbound packet. On a miss it loops up to
     MaximumEffortLevel (256) doing a HashSet<uint>.Add plus ConsumeKey (itself
     a second HashSet operation) per iteration - up to roughly 512 hash
     operations for ONE out-of-order or spoofed packet.

     That is an amplification worth rate-limiting. The common in-order case
     exits at the first `CurrentKey == x` compare, so instrument before
     optimising.

     See bugs-common.txt C.3, which describes how this same loop can saturate
     permanently and silently hang a session, and perf-network.txt P.12.

SECTION E - SUGGESTED PRIORITY
================================================================================
  1. bugs-common.txt C.1 (DerethDateTime) FIRST. It is the largest single cost
     in this project by a wide margin, it grows every year, and it sits on the
     landblock tick thread. It is filed as a bug rather than here only because
     it is an outright algorithmic defect.
  2. K.1  Hoist GetUnixTime out of the landblock loop. One line, and it fixes a
          timestamp-consistency smell at the same time.
  3. K.2  Single UtcNow snapshot in NetworkSession.Update. One line, removes
          hundreds of thousands of clock reads per second.
  4. K.3  Guard the instrumentation at the call sites. Coordinate with
          perf-managers.txt P.1-P.4 - it is the same cluster.
  5. K.5  Vectorise Hash32. Contained blast radius, bit-exact, and it sits on
          every packet in both directions.
  6. K.6  Instrument CryptoSystem.Search before doing anything to it.
  7. K.4  ThreadSafeRandom LAST despite being cheap to write - the RNG stream
          change and the inclusive-bounds contract make it the riskiest item
          here, and the win is the smallest.
